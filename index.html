Our Team Thesis

The thesis for our team is that Critical RFIs can be delayed when all requests are treated with similar priority, and AI-assisted impact assessment could reduce cost by 10% or more by helping GCs identify RFIs with the highest cost risk. According to an article in 2013, which looked at about 1,362 projects with an average of 796 RFI with a minimum of 100 RFI each, it cost them on average around $1,089 per request and about $859,000 total for the firms. That’s just looking at the ones that were reviewed and responded to. It’s not looking at the ones that got no reply, which was about 25% in the Americas (Hughes, et al., 2013). With almost 1 in 4 RFI not getting a response is not great. RFI are submitted when the general contractor or subcontractor is unsure of something and needs clarification and if they are not answered, it might lead to buildings that don’t meet the spec or aren’t good quality. The amount of money can really eat away from the profit margin of an already low margin industry, and that’s just talking about the cost of processing them alone. The amount of RFI also increases with the scale of the project and the amount of people who work on it. The bigger and more complex projects tend to have more RFI submitted per day and have a higher turnaround time for them. But, it is possible to have a shorter turnaround for complex projects if the team is experienced and organized. In a 2020 study on projects done in Australia and New Zealand, the average turnaround time was about 8.1 days. This is just the average, those with higher correspondences, about 156.6 or more per day, had a turnaround time of around 13.49 days. This is comparatively much higher turnaround times then the 5.97 days for projects with 16.9 or less correspondence a day. This effect is also seen with projects that last longer. The projects that lasted 819.4 days or more had a turnaround time of about 17.18 days compared to those that lasted 160.4 days or less with an average of about 1.79 days. The same thing happened with projects that had more people or organisations involved in it. The biggest projects are the ones that have the most RFIs and highest turnaround times (Aibinu et al., 2020). The projects that are the most costly and hard to build are the ones that are the most delayed. That amount of time could delay the schedule for the construction a lot, which would push the cost up by a lot, not to mention the missed deadlines. An AI-assisted impact assessment would be able to find the RFIs that would cause the highest cost risk so that we get to the most important RFIs the fastest. This is especially important with the bigger project, with the amount of RFIs they get and their turnaround time compared to smaller ones.

Project Lens

Our team will use California's High-Speed Rail Project as the main project data. We will focus on RFI records and any available information related to cost impact, change orders (CO). Metadata about relationships between RFIs and COs will also be used. These records will allow us to examine whether certain RFI characteristics are associated with higher financial impact and whether an AI-assisted system could identify those RFIs earlier.

If data is unavailable even after requesting from the California High-Speed Rail Projects' official site, we will use synthetic data from Kaggle, GitHub, or LLM generated.

Coordination Posture

Parties involved: General Contractor (GC), subcontractors, and the design team. The GC is the main user of the proposed system because it receives and coordinates RFIs across different project participants and must decide which issues need faster attention. Subcontractors often raise field questions or identify conflicts, while the design team provides clarification or design decisions.

Information artifacts in scope: RFIs, change orders, and available cost-impact records linked to project issues. RFI descriptions provide the initial problem context, while change orders and cost records can show whether certain types of RFIs later resulted in higher financial impact.

Demo 1: Build a basic RFI dashboard that organizes RFIs by status, trade, responsible party, and available cost-related information.

Demo 2: Develop an AI-assisted impact assessment that analyzes RFI descriptions and related project data to identify RFIs with potentially high financial impact.

Demo 3: Create a prioritization system that ranks RFIs by predicted cost risk so that the GC can review higher-risk RFIs earlier.

Demo 4: Compare AI-assisted prioritization with a standard review process and evaluate whether earlier identification of high-cost-risk RFIs could reduce avoidable RFI-related costs by at least 10%.
