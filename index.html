OUR TESTABLE CLAIM

Equal priority can hide
unequal cost risk.

Critical RFIs can be delayed when every request receives similar priority. We hypothesize that AI-assisted impact assessment can help GCs identify the highest cost risks earlier and reduce avoidable RFI-related costs by 10% or more.

Research target · Not a demonstrated result

Read our full thesis ↗ (opens in a new tab)
Read the full team thesis +

The thesis for our team is that construction projects can result in significant financial loss and project delays when Critical Requests For Information (RFI) are treated with similar priority without regard for cost importance. We propose that an AI-assisted impact assessment could reduce cost by 10% or more by helping general contractors (GC) identify RFIs with the highest cost risk. In 2013, a study of 1,362 construction projects in the Americas, surfaced an average of 796 RFIs with a minimum of 100 RFIs each, and an average cost of around $1,089 per request and about $859,000 total for the firms. This is considering only RFIs that were reviewed and responded to, and does not consider the cost of the estimated 25% that never got replies. (Hughes, et al., 2013). With almost 1 in 4 RFIs not getting a response, the current average RFI response rate becomes problematic. This lack of responses leaves the submitters unsure of their next steps, potentially leading to buildings that don’t meet the spec, or are otherwise low-quality. The cost of unsettled RFIs can eat away at the profit margin of an already low margin industry, and this is just considering the cost of processing them alone. The amount of RFIs increases with the scale of the project and the amount of people who work on it, meaning bigger and more complex projects tend to have more RFIs submitted per day in addition to having a higher turnaround time for them. However, it is possible to have a shorter turnaround for complex projects if the team is experienced and organized. In a 2020 study on projects done in Australia and New Zealand, the average RFI turnaround time was about 8.1 days, with those with higher correspondences having a about 156.6 or more per day, had a turnaround time of around 13.49 days. This is comparatively much higher turnaround times than the 5.97 days for projects with 16.9 or less correspondence a day. This effect is also seen with projects that last longer. The projects that lasted 819.4 days or more had a longer turnaround time of about 17.18 days compared to those that lasted 160.4 days or less with an average of about 1.79 days. Likewise, projects that had more people or organisations involved in it generally reported longer turnaround times (Aibinu et al., 2020). The projects that are the most costly and hard to build are the ones that are the most delayed, which would push the cost up for as long as the project remains incomplete, missing important deadlines in the process, resulting in increasingly severe losses financially and in terms of project completion. An AI-assisted impact assessment would be able to find the RFIs that would cause the highest cost risk so that we get to the most important RFIs the fastest. This would help reduce turnover times, buffering the impact of large collaboration populations and ensuring that no RFI is left unresponded to. This is especially important with the bigger project, with the amount of RFIs they get and their turnaround time compared to smaller ones.

04 / PROJECT LENS

Real complexity.
Traceable evidence.

Our intended project lens is California’s High-Speed Rail Project. We will examine RFI records, change orders, and linked cost-impact information to explore which early signals are associated with later financial impact.

Data access is still part of the plan.

If usable project records are unavailable, we will use synthetic data from Kaggle, GitHub, or LLM generation and document its assumptions and limits.
